The Foundation

What is On-Page SEO?

On-page SEO is the practice of optimizing individual web pages to rank higher on Google and earn more relevant traffic. It involves improving title tags, meta descriptions, header structure, content quality, internal links, image alt text, and URL structure - everything on your page that signals to Google what it's about and why it deserves to rank.

Here's the reality: you could have a fast, technically sound website with great backlinks - and still not rank. Why? Because if your page content doesn't clearly match what people are searching for, Google won't send them there.

This is what on-page SEO solves. It's about matching search intent - understanding exactly what someone typing a query into Google actually wants, and making sure your page delivers it better than anyone else on the first page.

Since Google's Helpful Content Update, this has become even more critical. Google now actively rewards pages written for real people and penalises thin, keyword-stuffed content written purely for search engines. Getting on-page SEO right means creating pages that genuinely help users - which is exactly what Google now rewards with rankings.

On-page SEO is also where E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) comes to life. Every element - from your author credentials to your content structure - sends trust signals that influence how Google evaluates your pages. I optimise all of these, consistently and correctly. How It Works

My On-Page SEO Audit Process

Here's the exact process I follow for every on-page SEO project - from first look to measurable results.

1

Website & Content Audit

I start by crawling your site with Screaming Frog and reviewing your Google Search Console data to identify pages with missing or duplicate title tags, weak meta descriptions, poor header structure, thin content, and broken internal links. This gives us a clear priority list before touching anything.

2

Keyword Mapping & Gap Analysis

Using Ahrefs and SEMrush, I map the right primary and secondary keywords to each page based on search intent and your site's current authority. I also identify keyword cannibalization - where multiple pages compete for the same term - and recommend consolidation or differentiation strategies.

3

Content & Search Intent Analysis

I compare your pages against the top 5–10 results currently ranking for your target keywords. What topics do they cover that you don't? What content format does Google prefer - lists, guides, short answers? What's in the People Also Ask section? This analysis shapes every content recommendation I make.

4

On-Page Optimization

With the audit and analysis complete, I implement the changes - rewriting title tags, improving meta descriptions, restructuring headers, optimizing content, adding ALT text, fixing URL slugs, building internal links, and aligning each page with its target intent. Every change is documented so you know exactly what was done and why.

5

Monitoring & Reporting

After changes go live, I monitor performance in Google Search Console - tracking impressions, clicks, CTR, and ranking positions for each optimized page. You receive a monthly report comparing before and after, with clear commentary on what's working and what we'll focus on next.

Your Questions Answered

On-Page SEO FAQ

Clear, straightforward answers - the kind I'd give a friend asking about their website over coffee.

What is on-page SEO and why does it matter?
On-page SEO is the process of optimizing everything on your web page so Google can understand it - and rank it. This includes your title tag, headers, content, internal links, images, and URL. It matters because even a technically perfect website with strong backlinks won't rank if Google can't tell what the page is about, or if the content doesn't match what people are actually searching for.
How do I improve on-page SEO for my website?
Here's what actually works, in priority order:
  1. Research and match your keywords to user search intent - not just volume.
  2. Write clear, keyword-informed title tags under 60 characters.
  3. Create unique meta descriptions that improve click-through rate.
  4. Use a proper H1 → H2 → H3 header hierarchy throughout the page.
  5. Write content that fully answers what the user came to find.
  6. Add internal links to related pages on your site.
  7. Add descriptive ALT text to every image.
  8. Check your page speed and Core Web Vitals scores.
  9. Monitor performance monthly in Google Search Console.
What are title tags and why are they so important?
A title tag is the blue clickable headline you see in Google search results. It's one of the strongest on-page ranking signals Google uses - it tells both the search engine and the user exactly what your page is about. A well-written title tag includes your primary keyword naturally, stays under 60 characters to avoid being cut off, and gives people a genuine reason to choose your result over the others on the page.
How long does on-page SEO take to show results?
For most websites, ranking improvements begin appearing within 4–8 weeks of on-page optimization. Some changes - like improving title tags and meta descriptions - can improve your click-through rate in as little as 1–2 weeks, even before your rankings shift significantly. Content improvements that better match search intent tend to show results within 6–10 weeks as Google re-evaluates the page. On-page SEO is one of the fastest ways to see organic improvements because you're fixing existing pages, not waiting to build new authority.
What's the difference between on-page SEO and off-page SEO?
On-page SEO is everything you do on your own website - content, tags, structure, images, and internal links. Off-page SEO is everything done outside your site to build authority, primarily through earning backlinks from other websites. Both are essential and work together: on-page SEO makes your pages rankable by giving Google the right signals; off-page SEO gives your pages the authority to compete.
